Research, model and evaluation

How fantasy cricket scores players, how this game prices and simulates them, and how to tell if a win predictor is any good. The evaluation below runs live in your browser.

How fantasy cricket works

Daily fantasy games such as Dream11 give each user a fixed credit budget (100 credits for 11 players), price players by expected output, and force role minimums so you can't stack one skill. Your score is the sum of what your players do in the real match.

The T20 points table this game borrows from:

  • Batting: +1 per run, +1 per four, +2 per six; +4 at 30, +8 at 50, +16 at 100; −2 for a duck (not for bowlers).
  • Bowling: +25 per wicket, +8 if bowled or LBW, +4 for 3 wickets, +8 for 4, +16 for 5.
  • Fielding: +8 per catch, +12 per stumping or direct-hit run out.

Five-for-Twenty shrinks this to five positions and $20, keeping the trade-off between stars and value picks.

How the model works

Each player gets a per-match simulator built from four career numbers per format: batting average, strike rate, bowling average and economy.

P(bats) = 0.95 BAT · 0.93 WK · 0.80 AR · 0.30 BOWL (T20) runs ~ Gamma(k=0.9, mean = avg × 0.85) boundary ≈ runs × 0.25 × SR/100 (fours/sixes bonus) overs = 4 BOWL · 3 AR (ODI: 10 · 7) wickets ~ Poisson(econ × overs / bowl avg) catches ~ Poisson(0.45, keepers 0.9)

The projection is the mean of 600 simulated matches. Price is a rank-preserving map of projection onto $2 to $7, in $0.5 steps, separately for T20I and ODI.

Win probability for a duel uses a normal approximation: P(A) = Φ((μA − μB) / √(σA² + σB²)), where μ and σ² are summed over each team's five players. A 4,000-match Monte Carlo is shown next to it as a check.

How to evaluate a win predictor

Score probabilities, not just picks. Accuracy only asks whether the favourite won. Proper scoring rules reward a forecast for being confident when it should be:

  • Brier score: mean of (p − outcome)². 0 is perfect; always saying 50% scores 0.25. Published T20 models land near 0.20 (logistic regression on IPL 2022 reached about 70% accuracy, Brier ≈ 0.20).
  • Log loss: −mean of log p(outcome). Punishes confident misses hard; a coin flip scores 0.693.
  • Calibration: of all games called at 70%, about 70% should be wins. A reliability diagram plots this; expected calibration error (ECE) is the weighted gap.

What the live eval shows. One fantasy match is mostly noise: a single wicket is worth 25 points, so even the best forecaster barely beats 50%. Over a 5- or 10-match series the gap in team quality shows through, and the Brier skill score climbs. That is why the head-to-head game defaults to a 5-match series.

Building a real backtest.

  1. Data: ball-by-ball files from Cricsheet give every T20I and ODI with player IDs.
  2. Split by time. Fit on 2016 to 2022, tune on 2023, test on 2024 onward. Never shuffle matches across years.
  3. Rebuild player ratings using only matches before each test game, to avoid leakage.
  4. Compare against baselines: coin flip, higher-ranked team, bookmaker odds.
  5. For the fantasy side, check Spearman rank correlation between projected and actual points, and the regret of your picked five against the best five in hindsight.
